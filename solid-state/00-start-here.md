# ELE 717 Solid State Electronics — Start Here

**Course:** ELE 717 Solid State Electronics (3 credits, compulsory), Postgraduate Diploma in Electrical/Electronic Engineering, Nigerian Defence Academy.

**Official content (NUC BMAS / CCMAS):** *Physics and material properties of semiconductors: elemental and compound materials, energy bands, carrier concentrations and statistics, high-field effects and velocity saturation. Carrier transport and injection: diffusion and drift currents, carrier lifetime, excess carriers, injection mechanisms, surface phenomena. Material growth and impurity control: bulk and epitaxial growth, purification of silicon, crystal growth, doping and impurity control. Junctions and interfaces: metal–semiconductor interfaces, Schottky barriers versus Ohmic contacts, stability and characterisation. Device technology: p-n junction diodes, BJTs and FETs/MOSFETs.*

This course takes you from "what is a semiconductor?" to "why does a diode obey the Shockley equation?". The issued lecture note covers the first part (materials, crystals, Miller indices, bonding). The examination paper goes much further, into growth, doping, band theory, carrier statistics, transport, recombination and junctions. Every one of those topics is taught here from scratch.

## Four ideas that carry the whole course

**1. The band gap decides everything.** A semiconductor has a gap E_g between a full valence band and an empty conduction band. It is small enough (about 0.5–3 eV) that heat, light or impurities can put carriers across it. Conductivity therefore becomes something you control, which is the whole point of the material.

**2. Two kinds of carrier.** Electrons in the conduction band and holes in the valence band both carry current. In equilibrium their product is fixed: **np = n_i²**. Doping raises one and lowers the other.

**3. Two ways to move.** Carriers drift in an electric field (J = qnμE) and diffuse down a concentration gradient (J = qD dn/dx). The two are linked by the Einstein relation D/μ = kT/q.

**4. Excess carriers die exponentially.** Minority carriers injected into a region recombine with a lifetime τ and travel a diffusion length L = √(Dτ) before they do. The diode equation is nothing more than this fact applied at the edge of a depletion region.

## How to study this

1. Read the module once, straight through.
2. Cover the solutions and attempt each exam question on paper.
3. Check your answer. For derivations, check that you stated the **assumptions**; examiners give marks for them.
4. Use Module 18 for the night before, and Module 19 to test yourself on all 50 questions in one sitting.

> The numbers you must know without looking: E_g (Si 1.12 eV, Ge 0.66 eV, GaAs 1.42 eV), n_i(Si) ≈ 1×10¹⁰ cm⁻³, kT/q = 0.0259 V at 300 K, q = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C, Si lattice constant 5.43 Å, μ_n(Si) ≈ 1350 cm²/V·s.

## Textbooks

From the lecture note: *Fundamentals of Solid State Electronics* (C.-T. Sah, World Scientific, 1991); *Solid State Electronic Circuits* (Edward W. Maby); *Solid State Electronic Devices* (B. G. Streetman and S. K. Banerjee). Further reading: Sze and Ng, *Physics of Semiconductor Devices*; Neamen, *Semiconductor Physics and Devices*; Pierret, *Semiconductor Device Fundamentals*; Kittel, *Introduction to Solid State Physics*.

Streetman and Banerjee is the closest match to the exam paper. Neamen is the easiest to read on your own.
