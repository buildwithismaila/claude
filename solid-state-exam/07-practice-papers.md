# Practice Examination Papers
<!-- group: Revision tools -->

Three full practice papers built from the 50 questions. Each question combines two or three of the 50, the way exam questions often do, and some parts change the numbers so you cannot simply recall an answer. The layout (eight questions, answer any five, 3 hours) is a practice format only: follow the instructions printed on your real paper.

## Symbols and abbreviations used on this page

Every symbol, constant and abbreviation on this page is listed here, even if an earlier week already defined it, so you can study this page on its own.

**Constants and units**

| Symbol | Meaning | Units / typical value |
|---|---|---|
| q | Magnitude of the electronic charge | 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C |
| k | Boltzmann constant | 8.617×10⁻⁵ eV/K = 1.381×10⁻²³ J/K |
| kT | Thermal energy | 0.0259 eV (often 0.02585 eV) at 300 K |
| kT/q | Thermal voltage | 0.0259 V (about 26 mV) at 300 K |
| ε_s (ε_si) | Permittivity of silicon, 11.7ε₀ | 1.04×10⁻¹² F/cm |
| eV | Electron-volt, a unit of energy | 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J |
| Å | Ångström, a unit of length | 1 Å = 10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.1 nm |
| μm | Micrometre (micron) | 1 μm = 10⁻⁴ cm = 10⁻⁶ m |

**Symbols**

| Symbol | Meaning | Units / typical value |
|---|---|---|
| E_g | Band-gap energy, E_c − E_v | eV; Si 1.12, Ge 0.66, GaAs 1.42 at 300 K |
| E_F | Fermi level: the energy at which a state has a 50 % chance of being occupied | eV |
| E_i | Intrinsic Fermi level, close to mid-gap | eV |
| f(E) | Fermi–Dirac function: probability that a state at energy E is occupied | dimensionless, 0 to 1 |
| N_c | Effective density of states in the conduction band | cm⁻³; Si 2.8×10¹⁹ at 300 K |
| N_v | Effective density of states in the valence band | cm⁻³; Si 1.04×10¹⁹ at 300 K |
| n | Electron concentration (free electrons per cm³) | cm⁻³ |
| p | Hole concentration (holes per cm³) | cm⁻³ |
| n_i | Intrinsic carrier concentration (n = p = n_i in pure material) | cm⁻³; Si ≈ 1×10¹⁰ at 300 K |
| N_D | Donor concentration | cm⁻³ |
| N_A | Acceptor concentration | cm⁻³ |
| λ | Wavelength | μm or nm; λ(μm) = 1.24/E(eV) |
| a | Lattice constant: edge length of the cubic unit cell | Å; Si 5.43, Ge and GaAs 5.65 |
| μ, μ_n, μ_p | Mobility: drift velocity per unit field (electrons, holes) | cm²/(V·s); Si μ_n 1350, μ_p 480 |
| J, J_n, J_p | Current density (total, electrons, holes) | A/cm² |
| D_n, D_p | Diffusion coefficients of electrons and holes | cm²/s; Si 35 and 12.4 |
| S | Surface recombination velocity | cm/s |
| V_bi | Built-in potential of a junction or Schottky contact | V; typically 0.5–1 V |
| W | Depletion-region width (in Week 13 W_B, W_E are base and emitter widths; in Week 14 W is channel width) | μm or cm |
| C, C_j | Capacitance (junction or depletion capacitance) | F (pF = 10⁻¹² F) |
| Φ_m | Work function of a metal | eV; Al 4.28, Au 5.1 |
| Φ_s | Work function of a semiconductor, χ + (E_c − E_F) | eV |
| χ | Electron affinity: energy from E_c up to the vacuum level | eV; Si 4.05 |
| Φ_B | Schottky barrier height seen from the metal | eV (or V) |

## Practice Paper 1

*Answer any FIVE questions. Each question carries 20 marks. Time: 3 hours.*

**1.** (a) Define a semiconductor and explain how its conductivity differs from that of a conductor and an insulator. *(8 marks)*
(b) Define Miller indices. *(4 marks)*
(c) A plane makes intercepts of a, 2b and ∞ (parallel to the z-axis) on the axes of a cubic silicon crystal. Find its Miller indices and the spacing between neighbouring planes of this family. *(8 marks)*

**2.** (a) Describe the major stages in converting metallurgical-grade silicon into electronic-grade silicon. Give the chemical equations. *(10 marks)*
(b) Explain the principle and operation of the Czochralski method. *(10 marks)*

**3.** (a) Explain the principle of ion implantation and its major advantages over thermal diffusion. *(10 marks)*
(b) Compare diffusion and ion implantation as doping techniques. *(10 marks)*

**4.** (a) What is meant by an intrinsic semiconductor? *(3 marks)*
(b) Derive the expression for the intrinsic carrier concentration. *(12 marks)*
(c) For GaAs at 300 K, N_c = 4.7×10¹⁷ cm⁻³, N_v = 7.0×10¹⁸ cm⁻³ and E_g = 1.42 eV. Calculate n_i. *(5 marks)*

**5.** (a) Explain how the Fermi level changes with doping in n-type and p-type semiconductors. Illustrate with band diagrams. *(10 marks)*
(b) Silicon is doped with N_A = 2×10¹⁵ cm⁻³ acceptors. Assuming complete ionisation, find the hole and electron concentrations at 300 K and the position of the Fermi level relative to E_i. *(10 marks)*

**6.** (a) Derive the expression for the drift current density due to electrons and holes. *(12 marks)*
(b) n-type silicon has n = 1×10¹⁵ cm⁻³. Calculate the electron drift current density in a field of 50 V/cm. *(8 marks)*

**7.** (a) Explain the Shockley–Read–Hall recombination mechanism. *(12 marks)*
(b) Define minority-carrier lifetime and explain its significance in semiconductor devices. *(8 marks)*

**8.** (a) Explain the formation of the depletion region and built-in potential in a p-n junction. *(10 marks)*
(b) A silicon p⁺n junction has N_A = 1×10¹⁸ cm⁻³ and N_D = 1×10¹⁵ cm⁻³. Calculate the built-in potential and the zero-bias depletion width at 300 K. *(10 marks)*

## Practice Paper 2

*Answer any FIVE questions. Each question carries 20 marks. Time: 3 hours.*

**1.** (a) Distinguish between elemental and compound semiconductors, giving two examples of each. *(8 marks)*
(b) Compare the properties and applications of Si, Ge and GaAs. *(12 marks)*

**2.** (a) What is a crystal lattice? Explain the significance of crystal structure in semiconductor materials. *(10 marks)*
(b) Explain the difference between covalent, ionic and metallic bonding, and identify the dominant bonding in silicon. *(10 marks)*

**3.** (a) State four advantages and two limitations of the Czochralski method. *(8 marks)*
(b) Compare VPE, LPE and MBE in terms of growth mechanism, temperature, precision and applications. *(12 marks)*

**4.** (a) What is doping, and why is it necessary in device fabrication? *(6 marks)*
(b) Differentiate between n-type and p-type semiconductors. *(6 marks)*
(c) Explain the difference between donor and acceptor impurities, with examples. *(8 marks)*

**5.** (a) State the time-independent Schrödinger equation and explain its terms. *(6 marks)*
(b) Explain the Kronig–Penney model and how it leads to energy bands. *(10 marks)*
(c) Distinguish between the valence band, the conduction band and the forbidden gap. *(4 marks)*

**6.** (a) Explain the Fermi–Dirac distribution function and its significance. *(8 marks)*
(b) Explain what happens to the distribution at absolute zero. *(4 marks)*
(c) Find the probability that a state 0.1 eV above the Fermi level is occupied at 300 K, and the probability that a state 0.1 eV below it is empty. *(8 marks)*

**7.** (a) Explain diffusion current in a semiconductor and state Fick's law. *(10 marks)*
(b) State and explain the Einstein relation. Use it to find the electron diffusion coefficient in a sample where μ_n = 1000 cm²/V·s at 300 K. *(10 marks)*

**8.** (a) Differentiate between a Schottky barrier diode and an ohmic contact, and explain their energy-band characteristics. *(14 marks)*
(b) Nickel (Φ_m = 5.15 eV) is deposited on n-type silicon with N_D = 1×10¹⁶ cm⁻³. Using the ideal model, find the barrier height Φ_B and the built-in potential V_bi. *(6 marks)*

## Practice Paper 3

*Answer any FIVE questions. Each question carries 20 marks. Time: 3 hours.*

**1.** (a) Explain why silicon is widely used in device fabrication. *(8 marks)*
(b) Explain the principles of VPE, LPE and MBE. *(12 marks)*

**2.** (a) Define Miller indices and explain how they identify crystal planes. *(8 marks)*
(b) Determine the Miller indices of a plane with intercepts 2a, 3b and 1c, and sketch the plane. *(12 marks)*

**3.** (a) Describe the mechanism of dopant diffusion in a semiconductor. *(10 marks)*
(b) State and explain Fick's first and second laws of diffusion. *(10 marks)*

**4.** (a) Explain the basic assumptions of the quantum-mechanical model of electrons in a semiconductor. *(8 marks)*
(b) Explain the difference between direct and indirect band-gap semiconductors. *(8 marks)*
(c) Calculate the longest wavelength of light that GaAs (E_g = 1.42 eV) can absorb or emit. *(4 marks)*

**5.** (a) Explain the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic carrier concentrations. *(6 marks)*
(b) Silicon has n_i = 1.0×10¹⁰ cm⁻³. If the electron and hole concentrations are equal, determine them. *(4 marks)*
(c) Define the density of states and explain its importance in finding carrier concentrations. *(10 marks)*

**6.** (a) Define carrier mobility and explain the factors that affect it. *(8 marks)*
(b) Explain the mechanisms of carrier scattering. *(6 marks)*
(c) Explain velocity saturation and hot-carrier effects. *(6 marks)*

**7.** (a) What is meant by generation and recombination of carriers? *(5 marks)*
(b) Explain the continuity equation for minority carriers and its application. *(9 marks)*
(c) What is surface recombination velocity? Explain how surface states affect carrier lifetime. *(6 marks)*

**8.** (a) Derive the ideal Shockley diode equation. *(12 marks)*
(b) A Schottky diode of area 1×10⁻³ cm² gives a straight line of 1/C² against V with slope −1.2×10²¹ F⁻²V⁻¹, crossing the voltage axis at +0.80 V. Find the doping concentration of the semiconductor and the built-in potential. *(8 marks)*
