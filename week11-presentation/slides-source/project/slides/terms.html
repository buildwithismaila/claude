Key terms

The energies that decide a contact

Vacuum level: energy of an electron at rest just outside the material. Everything is measured from it.

Fermi level: energy with a 50 % chance of being occupied. At equilibrium it is flat through materials in contact.

Work function: energy to lift an electron from the Fermi level to the vacuum level. Changes with doping.

Electron affinity: energy from the conduction-band edge to the vacuum level. Fixed for a material (Si: 4.05 eV).

Four energies decide what kind of contact we get. The vacuum level is our reference. The Fermi level is the 50 percent occupation level; the single most important rule today is that at equilibrium it is flat. The work function is measured from the Fermi level up to the vacuum level, so it changes when doping moves the Fermi level. The electron affinity is measured from the conduction-band edge, so it is fixed for a material: 4.05 eV for silicon. Note the conduction band starts at E_c and goes up; it is not a block reaching all the way to the vacuum level.
