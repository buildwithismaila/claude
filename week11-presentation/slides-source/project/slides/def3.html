The two contacts and their barriers

Schottky contact (Schottky barrier diode): a metal–semiconductor contact with an energy barrier to majority carriers, so it rectifies.

Ohmic contact: a metal–semiconductor contact with negligible resistance and a straight-line, symmetric current–voltage graph.

Rectifying: conducting easily in one direction (forward bias) and very little in the other (reverse bias).

Depletion region: a layer next to the interface emptied of free carriers, leaving fixed, charged dopant ions. Its width is W.

Accumulation layer: a thin layer at the interface holding extra majority carriers; the opposite of depletion.

Band bending: the curving of the band edges near the interface, caused by the charge and electric field there.

Barrier height: the energy an electron in the metal must gain to enter the semiconductor. It is fixed by the interface.

Built-in potential: the voltage across the depletion region at equilibrium: the barrier seen by electrons in the semiconductor.

The two kinds of contact. A Schottky contact has a barrier to majority carriers, so it rectifies: current flows easily one way only. An ohmic contact has negligible resistance and a straight-line I–V graph. A depletion region is a layer with no free carriers, only fixed ions; an accumulation layer is the opposite, with extra carriers. Band bending is how the band edges curve near the interface. There are two barriers to keep apart: the barrier height, seen by electrons in the metal and fixed by the interface; and the built-in potential, seen by electrons in the semiconductor.
