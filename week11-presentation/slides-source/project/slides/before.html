Before the materials touch, each has its own Fermi level. We line them up against the common vacuum level. For a metal like gold on n-type silicon, the metal work function is larger than the semiconductor's, so the semiconductor's Fermi level sits higher, by q times phi m minus phi s. Electrons always flow from the higher Fermi level to the lower one, so when we make contact they will flow from the semiconductor into the metal. Watch this happen in the animation on the next slide.
