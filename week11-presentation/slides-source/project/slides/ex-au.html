Worked example 1

Gold on n-type silicon

Given: gold (work function 5.10 eV) on n-type silicon with 1 × 10²² donors m⁻³. Find: the silicon work function, the barrier height and the built-in potential.

What this means: an electron in the gold must climb 1.05 eV, about 40 kT, so very few cross: the reverse current is small. Measured gold contacts give about 0.80 eV (see Fermi-level pinning).

Let us put numbers in. Gold has a work function of 5.10 eV. For silicon doped with 10 to the 22 donors per cubic metre, E_c minus E_F is 0.02585 times ln of 2800, which is 0.205 eV, so the silicon work function is 4.26 eV. Gold's is larger, so we get a Schottky barrier. The barrier height is 5.10 minus 4.05, 1.05 eV, and the built-in potential is 5.10 minus 4.26, 0.84 V. Point out that the barrier height and built-in potential differ by exactly E_c minus E_F, 0.21 eV. In practice gold on silicon measures about 0.8 eV, which we explain shortly.
