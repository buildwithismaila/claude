Six things to take away

  1. At equilibrium the Fermi level is flat; electrons flow from the higher Fermi level to the lower one.
  2. On n-type silicon, a metal with the larger work function forms a Schottky barrier: depletion region, bands bent up.
  3. Barrier height from the metal: work function minus electron affinity. Built-in potential: the difference of work functions.
  4. Bias changes only the semiconductor-side barrier; the metal-side barrier stays fixed.
  5. Current is thermionic emission of majority carriers: fast, low turn-on voltage, more leakage.
  6. Ohmic contacts come from accumulation, or from heavy doping that makes the barrier thin enough to tunnel through.

To sum up in six points. If you remember only one thing, remember that the Fermi level is flat at equilibrium: every band diagram today follows from it. Real barriers are also set largely by Fermi-level pinning, so use measured barrier heights when they are given.
