Under applied bias

Bias moves only the semiconductor side

Rule: the barrier from the metal stays fixed. Forward bias lowers the semiconductor-side barrier and narrows the depletion region; reverse bias raises it and widens it.

Now apply a voltage. The two sides are no longer in equilibrium, so there is no single flat Fermi level: the semiconductor's Fermi level moves by qV relative to the metal's. The key rule: the barrier seen from the metal, q phi Bn, is fixed by the interface and does not change. The applied voltage drops across the depletion region and changes only the semiconductor-side barrier. Forward bias, metal positive, raises the semiconductor Fermi level, lowers the bending to q times V bi minus V, and narrows W, so electrons pour over into the metal. Reverse bias, metal negative, raises the bending to q times V bi plus V R and widens W, so that flow is cut off.
