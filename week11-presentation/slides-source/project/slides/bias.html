Under applied bias

Bias moves only the semiconductor side

Rule: applied voltage V = (metal potential) − (semiconductor potential). The barrier from the metal stays fixed; only the semiconductor-side barrier and the width change.

The three panels appear one at a time. The applied voltage V is the metal's potential minus the semiconductor's, so forward bias means the metal is positive. First, equilibrium: one flat Fermi level and band bending q V bi. Second, forward bias: the semiconductor's Fermi level rises by qV, the bending falls to q times V bi minus V, W narrows, and electrons pour over into the metal. Third, reverse bias: the semiconductor's Fermi level falls, the bending rises to q times V bi plus V R, W widens, and that flow stops. In all three, the barrier from the metal, q phi Bn, is the same: it is fixed by the interface. At large reverse voltage the diode breaks down, at a lower voltage than a p–n diode.
