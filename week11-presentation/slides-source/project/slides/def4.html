Current, bias and real contacts

Forward bias: the metal made positive relative to n-type semiconductor; it lowers the semiconductor-side barrier.

Reverse bias: the metal made negative; it raises the semiconductor-side barrier.

Thermionic emission: carriers crossing over a barrier using their thermal energy.

Tunnelling (field emission): carriers passing through a barrier only a few nanometres thick: a quantum effect.

Saturation current: the small current that flows under reverse bias; it sets the size of the forward current.

Ideality factor (η): how closely a diode follows the ideal equation: 1 for ideal, 1.0–1.1 for a good Schottky diode.

Richardson constant: the material constant in the thermionic-emission current; about 1.1 × 10⁶ A m⁻² K⁻² for n-type silicon.

Fermi-level pinning: surface states holding the Fermi level at the interface near a fixed energy, so the barrier hardly depends on the metal.

Specific contact resistance: contact resistance times contact area (Ω m²); good ohmic contacts reach 10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻¹² Ω m².

Silicide: a compound of a metal and silicon (TiSi₂, NiSi) used to make stable, low-resistance contacts.

Last set of definitions. Forward bias makes the metal positive on n-type silicon and lowers the barrier from the semiconductor side; reverse bias does the opposite. Carriers cross a barrier either over it, thermionic emission, or through it, tunnelling. The saturation current is the small reverse current, and the ideality factor tells us how ideal a diode is. The Richardson constant appears in the current equation. Fermi-level pinning explains why real barrier heights hardly depend on the metal. Ohmic contacts are rated by their specific contact resistance, and silicides are the compounds used to make good ones on silicon.
