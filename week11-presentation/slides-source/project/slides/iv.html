The Schottky diode's saturation current is typically thousands to millions of times larger than a p–n diode's, so it turns on at a lower voltage: about 0.3 V on silicon instead of 0.7 V. That means less power lost when it conducts, which is why Schottky rectifiers are used in switching power supplies. The price is higher reverse leakage and a lower breakdown voltage. Because there is no stored minority charge, there is no reverse-recovery delay. The dashed line is an ohmic contact for comparison: a straight line through the origin.
