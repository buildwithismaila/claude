Work function of doped silicon

Given: n-type silicon with 1 × 10²² donors m⁻³; effective density of states 2.8 × 10²⁵ m⁻³; electron affinity 4.05 eV; kT = 0.02585 eV at 300 K. Find: the work function.

Step 1: how far the Fermi level lies below the conduction band

Step 2: add the electron affinity

What this means: any metal with a work function above 4.26 eV (gold, nickel, platinum) tends to form a Schottky barrier on this silicon.

Our first number. For silicon with 10 to the 22 donors per cubic metre, E_c minus E_F is kT times ln of N_c over N_D: 0.02585 times ln of 2800, which is 0.02585 times 7.94, giving 0.205 eV. Add the electron affinity, 4.05 eV, to get the work function: 4.26 eV. We will use this silicon for the rest of the talk. Any metal with a larger work function, such as gold at 5.10 eV, will form a Schottky barrier on it.
