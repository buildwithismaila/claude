The Schottky–Mott rule is the ideal case. Real barriers depend much less on the metal. The chart compares the ideal values, phi m minus chi, with typical measured values on n-type silicon from Sze and Ng: ideal values spread from 0.23 to 1.60 eV, measured ones only from 0.50 to 0.90 eV. The reason is interface states, dangling bonds and defects with energies in the gap, which pin the Fermi level at the surface. On gallium arsenide almost every metal gives about 0.8 eV. A second, smaller effect is image-force lowering: an electron near the metal is attracted by its image charge, which lowers the barrier slightly, about 25 millivolts for our gold example, and more under reverse bias.
