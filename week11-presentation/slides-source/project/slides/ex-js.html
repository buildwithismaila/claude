Formulas first. The saturation current density is A double-star T squared e to the minus q phi B over kT. For forward currents much larger than J s, the minus one in the current equation can be dropped, so V equals kT over q times ln of J over J s. Substitute: J s is 1.1 times 10 to the 6 times 300 squared times e to the minus 0.80 over 0.0259, which is 9.9 times 10 to the 10 times 3.85 times 10 to the minus 14, or 3.8 times 10 to the minus 3 amperes per square metre. For 10 to the 4 amperes per square metre, V is 0.0259 times ln of about 2.6 million, which is 14.8, giving 0.38 V. A silicon p–n diode needs about 0.7 V.
