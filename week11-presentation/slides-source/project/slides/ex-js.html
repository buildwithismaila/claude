Worked example 5

Saturation current and turn-on voltage

Given: measured barrier height 0.80 eV on n-type silicon, Richardson constant 1.1 × 10⁶ A m⁻² K⁻², T = 300 K, η = 1. Find: the saturation current density, and the voltage for 1 × 10⁴ A m⁻².

What this means: the Schottky diode carries 1 A per cm² at only 0.38 V; a silicon p–n diode needs about 0.7 V. Less voltage means less power lost: why Schottky rectifiers are used.

This example shows where the low turn-on voltage comes from. With a measured barrier of 0.80 eV, J s is 1.1 times 10 to the 6, times 300 squared, times e to the minus 0.80 over 0.0259: that is 9.9 times 10 to the 10 times 3.85 times 10 to the minus 14, or 3.8 times 10 to the minus 3 amperes per square metre. For a current density of 10 to the 4 amperes per square metre, one ampere per square centimetre, the forward voltage is kT over q times ln of J over J s: 0.0259 times 14.8, or 0.38 V. A silicon p–n diode needs about 0.7 V for the same current.
