Panel (a) is our Schottky contact again: a thick barrier that electrons can only cross by thermionic emission. There are three ways to make an ohmic contact instead. Route 1, panel (c): choose a metal with a work function smaller than the semiconductor's. Electrons then flow into the semiconductor, accumulate at the surface, the bands bend down and there is no barrier. In practice Fermi-level pinning makes this unreliable on silicon and gallium arsenide. Route 2, panel (b), the practical one: dope very heavily under the contact. The barrier height is the same, but the barrier becomes a few nanometres thin and electrons tunnel through it in both directions. Route 3: surface cleaning and annealing to form a silicide, combined with route 2. Contacts are judged by their specific contact resistance, resistance times area.
