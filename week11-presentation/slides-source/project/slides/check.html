1. Platinum (work function 5.65 eV) is deposited on n-type silicon. What is the ideal barrier height, and why is the measured value only about 0.9 eV?

2. Why does a Schottky diode switch faster than a p–n diode?

3. A contact on n-type silicon has 0.60 V across its depletion region and 10²⁴ donors m⁻³. How thick is the barrier? Is that thin enough for an ohmic contact?

Answers. 1: ideal barrier height = 5.65 minus 4.05 = 1.60 eV; the measured value is about 0.9 eV because interface states pin the Fermi level at the surface. 2: a Schottky diode conducts by majority carriers only, so no minority charge is stored and there is no reverse-recovery delay. 3: W = 2.79 times 10 to the minus 7 metres divided by the square root of 100, which is 2.8 times 10 to the minus 8 metres, 28 nanometres. That is still too thick for strong tunnelling; the doping must be raised to about 10 to the 25 per cubic metre, which gives 8.8 nanometres. Then invite questions from the class.
