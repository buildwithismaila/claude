Worked example 6

Forward current of a Schottky diode

Given: ideal diode (η = 1), saturation current 2 μA, forward voltage 0.20 V, T = 300 K, kT/q = 0.02585 V. Find: the current.

What this means: just 0.2 V raises the current to 4.58 mA, over two thousand times the saturation current. Each further 0.1 V multiplies it by about 48.

A worked example from our group's notes. The saturation current is 2 microamps and we apply 0.20 V forward. The exponent is 0.20 divided by 0.02585, which is 7.737, and e to the 7.737 is 2291. Subtract one and multiply by 2 microamps: 4.58 milliamps. Note the Boltzmann constant must be 1.381 times 10 to the minus 23 joules per kelvin if you work from q, k and T separately; it is simpler to use kT over q equals 0.02585 V. In a real diode, series resistance and an ideality factor above one would make the current somewhat smaller.
