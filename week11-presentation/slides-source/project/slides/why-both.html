Why devices need both, and keep them stable

Both are needed

  • A Schottky diode: rectifying contact on top, ohmic contact underneath.
  • The Schottky contact gives the rectification; the ohmic contact lets current in and out with no extra barrier or voltage drop.
  • In a solar cell every contact must be ohmic; an accidental barrier adds resistance and costs power.

Stability of contacts

  • Aluminium spiking into shallow junctions: prevented by 1 % Si in the Al, or a TiN barrier.
  • Electromigration of the metal at high current density.
  • Native oxide or reactions at the interface change the barrier and the contact resistance.

A practical device uses both kinds of contact together. In a Schottky diode the top contact rectifies and the bottom one is ohmic, so the current can get out without a second barrier. In a solar cell every contact must be ohmic: an unintended rectifying contact adds series resistance, lowers the fill factor and reduces the output power. Contacts must also stay stable through hot processing and years of use: aluminium can spike into shallow junctions, metal can migrate under high current, and oxides or reactions at the interface change the barrier height and contact resistance.
