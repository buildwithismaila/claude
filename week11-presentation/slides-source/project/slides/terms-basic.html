Key terms, part 1

The materials and the words we will use

Metal

Very many free electrons, 10²⁸–10²⁹ m⁻³; states filled up to the Fermi level.

Semiconductor

Conductivity between metal and insulator, set by doping, temperature and light.

Doping

Donors (phosphorus) give electrons: n-type. Acceptors (boron) give holes: p-type.

Majority carrier

The carrier in the majority: electrons in n-type, holes in p-type.

Band gap

Energy from the valence-band top to the conduction-band bottom. Si: 1.12 eV.

Depletion region

A layer emptied of free carriers, holding only fixed, charged dopant ions.

Built-in potential

The voltage across the depletion region at equilibrium.

Emission, tunnelling

Crossing over a barrier using heat; passing through a barrier a few nm thick.

Before the physics, the words. A metal has a huge density of free electrons, ten to the 28 or 29 per cubic metre, filled up to its Fermi level. A semiconductor's conductivity is controlled by doping: donors such as phosphorus give electrons and make it n-type; acceptors such as boron give holes and make it p-type. The majority carrier is the one there is more of. The band gap is 1.12 eV in silicon. A depletion region is a layer with no free carriers, only fixed ions; the voltage across it at equilibrium is the built-in potential. Finally, carriers cross a barrier in two ways: over the top by thermionic emission, or straight through a very thin barrier by tunnelling.
