Why heavy doping makes a contact ohmic

Given: 0.60 V across the depletion region of a contact on n-type silicon. Find: the depletion width for 10²² and for 10²⁵ donors m⁻³.

What this means: same barrier height, 31.6 times thinner. Nothing tunnels through 279 nm, but electrons pass freely through 8.8 nm: the contact is now ohmic.

This example, from our group notes, shows route 2 in numbers. With 0.6 V across the depletion region and 10 to the 22 donors per cubic metre, W is 279 nanometres: far too thick to tunnel through, so the contact rectifies. Raise the doping a thousand times, to 10 to the 25, and W falls by the square root of a thousand, 31.6, to 8.8 nanometres. Electrons now tunnel straight through. Nothing about the barrier height changed: the thinness alone makes the contact ohmic.
