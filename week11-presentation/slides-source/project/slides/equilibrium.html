ELE 717 · Week 11 · Metal–semiconductor interfaces

9 / 28

At equilibrium there are two barriers. From the metal side, an electron must climb from the Fermi level to the conduction-band edge at the interface: that is the barrier height, q phi Bn, equal to q times phi m minus chi. This is the Schottky–Mott rule. It follows because the vacuum level is continuous at the interface. From the semiconductor side, an electron climbs only the band bending, q V bi, equal to the original difference in work functions. For p-type the barrier is for holes, E_g minus q times phi m minus chi, so for the same metal the two barriers add up to the band gap. Remember: the barrier height is fixed by the interface.
