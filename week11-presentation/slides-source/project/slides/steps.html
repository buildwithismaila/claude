Forming a Schottky barrier

What happens on contact, step by step

1

Electrons flow from the semiconductor into the metal

2

They leave behind fixed, positive donor ions

3

A depletion region forms; a sheet of electrons sits on the metal

4

An electric field points from the ions towards the metal

5

The bands bend upward towards the interface

6

Flow stops when the Fermi levels align

ELE 717 · Week 11 · Metal–semiconductor interfaces

8 / 28

Here is the animation in words, the six steps examiners look for. One: electrons flow from the semiconductor into the metal. Two: each leaves a positive donor ion that is locked in the crystal. Three: those ions form a depletion region, balanced by a thin sheet of extra electrons on the metal surface. Four: positive ions and negative sheet create an electric field. Five: the field bends the bands upward. Six: the flow stops when the Fermi levels are equal. Electrons still cross both ways, but the two flows balance, so the net current is zero. This is the ideal Schottky–Mott picture, with no interface states.
